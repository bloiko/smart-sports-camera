# Competitive landscape & market: AI auto-tracking sports cameras and phone-based auto-filming apps (as of 10 Oct 2026)

Note: research done 10 Oct 2026 with WebSearch/WebFetch. Prices are "seen 10 Oct 2026" unless another date is given. Some vendor pages hide prices behind quotes or JS placeholders, and those are marked as gaps.

## 1. Competitors: products, hardware vs phone, prices, subscriptions, sports, streaming

### Takeaway
The market splits into three tiers:
- **Dedicated panoramic AI cameras with mandatory subscriptions:** Veo, Pixellot, Hudl Focus, Spiideo, Trace, Reeplayer. Hardware costs about $1,000 or more, plus about $45–250/month.
- **Low-cost phone-holder hardware with no or light subscriptions:** XbotGo Chameleon at about $300, BallerCam at $299, and gimbal + app combos such as FoloCam and Insta360 DockKit.
- **Software that reuses existing cameras or phones:** Veo Go (2 iPhones, no live), Once Sport Autocam (any wide camera, YouTube live), and Balltime (volleyball, phone footage).

### Cited Findings

**Veo (Denmark): dedicated camera, subscription required**
- Veo Cam costs €999 one-time, excluding VAT. One subscription is required per camera. Plans are Starter (10 rec h/month, 3–6 months storage), Team (40 h), Club (200 h, 5 teams) and Enterprise (600 h, 20 teams). The FAQ gives €45–€165/month "depending on team size". Billing can be monthly, 6-monthly, yearly or 2-yearly. — [Veo shop EU](https://shop.veo.co/)
- In the US, Veo Cam is $999 and the subscription is about $84–$250/month. Plans are Family, Team, Club and Enterprise. — [Veo US shop](https://us.shop.veo.co/)
- Live streaming and Veo Analytics are add-ons listed as "From €0/per camera/per month". Live streaming works for football only and needs a separate data plan. — [Veo shop EU](https://shop.veo.co/)
- A 2026 promo offered "$400 off plus free Veo Live" with Cam 3, tripod and a 12-month Team/Club/Enterprise plan. The subscription auto-renews at the standard rate, and the offer expired 15 Jun 2026. — [Veo end-of-school offer 2026](https://www.veo.com/en-us/end-of-school-offer-2026)
- **Veo Go** is Veo's own phone-only product:
  - It uses 2 iPhones with ultra-wide cameras (iPhone 11 or newer, excluding SE and 16e; iOS 17+), each filming half the pitch.
  - Footage is uploaded after the match. The cloud stitches it and produces an "AI follow-cam".
  - It requires a subscription. The yearly plan includes a phone rig and tripod. Limits are 20 rec h/month, 15 users and 6 months storage.
  - It does **not** support live streaming, Veo Analytics or Player Spotlight. Full matches can't be downloaded, and it is iPhone-only.
  - Sports listed are football, basketball, rugby and lacrosse, with smart highlights for football only.
  - It needs about 6 GB of phone storage per hour. — [Veo Go product page](https://www.veo.com/product/veo-go)
- The Veo Go App Store listing has a rating of 3.8 from only 4 ratings. One complaint: the app was blocked on the iPhone 17 family with a "no ultra-wide camera" error. — [App Store: Veo Go](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6475045572)

**Pixellot (Israel): dedicated camera (Air NXT, fixed Show/Prime), B2B-heavy**
- Pixellot Air NXT is $1,149 at retailer Videoguys. It has dual 12 MP sensors (4056×3040) with a 200° FOV at 25/30 fps, Wi-Fi/4G/5G/Ethernet, 512 GB storage, IP65 and a 1.8 kg weight. Live streaming is supported. — [Videoguys Air NXT](https://videoguys.com/products/pixellot-air-nxt-portable-ai-sports-camera)
- The Pixellot "You" consumer plans are monthly, 1-year and 2-year, in Team 1, Team Club and Club Unlimited tiers. Prices were not visible on the old pricing page. There is also a bring-your-own GoPro option for baseball and volleyball. — [Pixellot You pricing (old page)](https://pixellotyou.wpengine.com/pricing-old/)
- Air sports listed are basketball, soccer, ice hockey, volleyball, American football, lacrosse and field hockey. A promo offered 3 months free on the monthly plan (page modified Jan 2024). — [Pixellot You Air offer](https://you.pixellot.tv/february-air-offer)

**Hudl Focus Flex (US): dedicated camera, subscription required**
- The price is by sales quote only. A yearly Hudl Focus subscription is required, and "you own the camera but need an active subscription to use it".
- It uses AI player tracking for pan and zoom, mainly for soccer and hockey.
- It streams via 4G (US-only data plan, sold separately) or Wi-Fi to Hudl TV.
- It has 500 GB of storage, and setup requires calibration in the Focus app. — [Hudl Focus Flex FAQ](https://www.hudl.com/products/focus/faq/portable)

**Trace (US): own camera with lease, parent-paid subscription**
- PlayerFocus Basic is $15/month and PlayerFocus Pro is $25/month, both billed annually. These were last seen in June 2026.
- An "As a Team" plan was added 4 Aug 2026: $200/year per family, or $1,400/year for 7, dropping to $175 each from 11+ families. It includes a **free camera lease**.
- The PricingSaaS data has inconsistencies. — [PricingSaaS: Trace](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/traceup)
- The Trace Teams app has a 4.8 rating from about 4.1K ratings. Soccer only. Complaints include:
  - a membership is now required to watch games
  - upload failures
  - recording starts early
  - email-only, slow support
  - the camera occasionally misses key moments — [App Store: Trace Teams](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1351001083)

**Reeplayer (US): own camera, "all-inclusive plan"**
- Reeplayer Pro, a viewer-side app plan, costs $4.99/month or $39.99/year and is included for camera owners. The camera's AI "zooms and follows the action automatically". Pro adds pinch-zoom, live replay and casting. — [Reeplayer help: Pro pricing](https://intercom.help/reeplayer/en/articles/16400906-reeplayer-pro-what-s-included-and-what-it-costs)
- The camera is a custom US-built device sold under an "all-inclusive plan". No price was published on the help page. — [Reeplayer help](https://reeplayer.helpscoutdocs.com/article/23-what-s-the-difference-between-reeplayer-and-others)

**Spiideo (Sweden): fixed installs, pro/semi-pro B2B**
- Products are Spiideo Play (cloud broadcasting and streaming) and Spiideo Perform (multi-angle analysis), plus cloud-native AI cameras. It is in over 6,000 venues, used by over 4,000 teams, and present in 60+ countries. — [SVG, Jun 2024](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2024/06/06/ai-powered-swedish-sport-tech-spiideo-raises-20-million-in-growth-round-led-by-germanys-cipio-partners/)

**XbotGo (China/US): phone-holder gimbal or all-in-one camera, no subscription**
- Chameleon is a smartphone-holder AI gimbal at $299.99 (compare-at $369). Falcon is an all-in-one 4K camera at $799 with no subscription. There is no price yet for "AI Analysis". (Page dated 21 Jul 2026.) — [XbotGo: Trace vs Veo](https://xbotgo.com/blogs/buying-guide/trace-vs-veo)
- Chameleon streams to YouTube, Facebook Live, XbotGo Live or any RTMP service. The vendor claims 20+ sports, 20 GB free cloud, no monthly fees, and 90–95% soccer tracking success (vendor claim, unverified). — [XbotGo Chameleon review (vendor blog, 14 Jun 2025)](https://xbotgo.com/fr/blogs/buying-guide/xbotgo-chameleon-review)

**BallerCam (Baller Inc., US): clip-on 180° lens + iPhone app, virtual crop**
- The hardware is $299 (sale price, regular $349) and requires an iPhone 13 Pro or newer.
- Plans: Base is included (20 h cloud), Starter is $99/year (100 h), Pro is $199/year (250 h). "No subscription required."
- About 5 Mbps upload is recommended for streaming. — [BallerCam product page](https://ballercam.com/products/ballercam)
- How it works:
  - It captures the full field in 4K through the wide lens, then **digitally pans, tilts and zooms** using on-device AI.
  - **Livestream is 720p**, and local recording is 1080p.
  - The company admits "the more you zoom in … softening or blur".
  - Tracking depends on tripod height, framing and masking. — [BallerCam support: video quality](https://support.ballercam.com/hc/en-us/articles/38730043045517-What-s-the-video-quality-like-on-BallerCam)
- The app supports basketball, soccer/futsal, volleyball and flag football. Live links don't require an account. It has a 4.9 rating from 861 ratings.
- Complaints include:
  - multi-device time sync
  - poor connection degrading recordings
  - clips pointing to the wrong segment
  - the PC desktop viewer not loading
  - requests for pausing the stream and faster startup — [App Store: BallerCam](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6747689741)
- This is the closest analogue to the target concept (4K wide capture → AI crop → stream), but it needs a proprietary lens accessory and is iPhone-only.

**FoloCam (indie, US): phone app + DockKit gimbal (physical tracking)**
- It is iPhone-only (iOS 26+) and requires a DockKit gimbal (Insta360 Flow Pro/Flow 2 Pro, DJI Osmo Mobile 8, etc.). It does AI ball tracking for basketball and soccer with 4K recording.
- Premium is $49.99/year, mainly to remove the watermark.
- It has 3 ratings (5.0), and v1.0 was released 11 Mar 2026. — [App Store: FoloCam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6759841426)

**Insta360 Flow 2 Pro (DockKit gimbal)**
- It costs $159 and has DockKit tracking in the native iPhone camera. The review shows person/pet tracking only and doesn't cover ball or field-sport tracking. — [GimbalReview, 10 Jun 2025](https://www.gimbalreview.com/insta360-flow-2-pro-review-2025/)

**Once Sport Autocam (EU, EUR pricing): software turning any wide footage into a virtual-camera broadcast**
- Setup options:
  - Fixed: 2 cameras plus a mini PC, about €1,200 + VAT.
  - Portable: any whole-field camera, such as a dual GoPro (about €600), Insta360 Ace 2 or Reolink Duo 3. On small pitches, "an iPhone with a wide lens can be enough".
- Plans are Basic (40 h/month) and Pro (unlimited), with processing on your own PC. Alternatively, cloud processing costs **€5 per game** with results within 24 h. Subscription prices were not shown.
- It does **live streaming to YouTube** or any platform, and has a stream scheduler.
- Sports: football, basketball, futsal, handball, field hockey, American football and rugby. — [Once Sport Autocam](https://once.sport/autocam)
- This is the closest *software* analogue to the target concept, but it is desktop/cloud-centric, not a polished phone app.

**Balltime (volleyball, acquired by Hudl)**
- The AI platform works from smartphone or any-device footage. It is freemium, with enhanced features at $29.99/month.
- It had about $100K MRR (estimate) when sold for an "8-figure" sum. — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown)

**Market report listings of key players**
- Market.us lists Pixellot, Spiideo, Veo, WSC Sports, Wicket, Trace and SwingVision, among big-tech names. — [Market.us summary](https://scoop.market.us/sports-auto-camera-ai-market-news/)
- Lucintel lists Pixellot, Trace, Spiideo, Hudl Focus Flex, TeamTV, Hi Rise Camera, SVT Advantage, MyPlay, Veo and Reeplayer. — [Lucintel](https://lucintel.com/soccer-camera-market.aspx)

### Inferences
- **Phone-only with a ball-following virtual camera AND live streaming is effectively unoccupied by a polished mass-market app:**
  - Veo Go is phone-only but needs 2 iPhones, has no live streaming and requires a subscription.
  - BallerCam does crop and stream, but needs a $299 lens and an iPhone 13 Pro+, and streams at 720p.
  - FoloCam and XbotGo do physical tracking and need a gimbal.
  - Once Sport is software, but PC/cloud-oriented with a 24 h cloud turnaround.
- An Android-first or cross-platform phone-only product would face almost no direct competition. Every phone-centric competitor found (Veo Go, BallerCam, FoloCam) is iPhone-only.
- Price anchors for a phone-only product:
  - consumer apps: $40–100/year (FoloCam $49.99/year, BallerCam $99–199/year, Reeplayer Pro $39.99/year)
  - pay-per-game: €5 (Once Sport)
  - incumbents, by comparison: Veo €45–165 or $84–250/month on top of about €/$999 hardware
- A single phone's wide lens limits virtual zoom quality. BallerCam openly admits blur when zoomed. The key technical risk is resolution on full-size 11v11 pitches, which is why Veo Go uses 2 phones and Once Sport suggests dual GoPros for big fields.

### Gaps
- Exact per-plan prices for Veo (each tier), Pixellot Air subscription, Hudl Focus, Spiideo, Reeplayer camera and Once Sport subscriptions were not visible (quote-based or JS-rendered pages).
- Veo Go's actual subscription price is a placeholder on its product page.
- Not researched or not found: SoloShot (current status), Pivo (sports tracking), Insta360 Link, PlayOn hardware, Playsight current pricing, "AutoCam" by Baller beyond BallerCam, Sportscam.
- Google Play phone-only competitors: searches surfaced none. Android landscape not verified directly on Google Play.

## 2. Which products are pure software / phone-only with automatic ball-following virtual camera? How good are they?

### Takeaway
Only Veo Go (2 iPhones, post-match cloud, no live) and Once Sport Autocam (any camera, including iPhone on small pitches) are truly hardware-free virtual-camera products. BallerCam is "nearly phone-only" (needs a clip lens). Quality signals are thin: Veo Go has 4 ratings, and BallerCam is well rated but with sync and connection complaints and a 720p live stream.

### Cited Findings
- Veo Go: 2 iPhones, AI follow-cam produced in the cloud after upload, no live, iPhone-only, about 6 GB/hour per phone. — [Veo Go](https://www.veo.com/product/veo-go)
- Veo Go has a 3.8 App Store rating from 4 ratings, with an iPhone 17 incompatibility complaint. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6475045572)
- Once Sport: a neural-net digital pan/zoom over panorama footage, with YouTube live and €5/game cloud processing. — [Once Sport](https://once.sport/autocam)
- BallerCam: 4K wide capture with on-device AI crop, 720p live and 1080p local, with a 4.9★ rating from 861 ratings. — [Support](https://support.ballercam.com/hc/en-us/articles/38730043045517-What-s-the-video-quality-like-on-BallerCam); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6747689741)
- FoloCam tracks the ball, but via a physical DockKit gimbal rather than a virtual crop, and has 3 ratings. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6759841426)

### Inferences
- The space is technically validated (Veo itself shipped a phone product, and BallerCam proves on-device crop + stream works on iPhone), but no one offers single-phone, no-accessory, live 1080p crop to YouTube on both iOS and Android.
- Veo Go's design choice (2 phones, no live) suggests Veo judged a single phone insufficient for full-pitch quality. This is a risk to validate, perhaps by targeting small-sided, futsal, youth and basketball first.

### Gaps
- No independent reviews or benchmarks of tracking accuracy found for any phone-only product.
- Reddit threads were not reachable through the searches used.

## 3. Market size estimates and growth

### Takeaway
Analyst estimates are small and inconsistent:
- AI sports auto-camera market: about $486M (2024), growing to about $5.2B by 2034 at 26.7% CAGR (Market.us).
- Broader sports analytics: about $2.3B (2025), growing to $4.75B by 2030 at 15.7% CAGR (MarketsandMarkets).
- Soccer cameras: 7.9% CAGR (Lucintel).

Operational metrics from Pixellot show big real volume in US school streaming.

### Cited Findings
- **Sports Auto-Camera AI market:**
  - USD 486.3M (2024), forecast USD 5,184.1M (2034), CAGR 26.7%.
  - North America has a 45.8% share (USD 222.7M).
  - Segments: fixed, portable and drone-based. — [Market.us (summary)](https://scoop.market.us/sports-auto-camera-ai-market-news/)
- **Sports analytics market:** USD 2.29B (2025), forecast USD 4.75B (2030), CAGR 15.7% (MarketsandMarkets, Jul 2025). **AI in Sports:** USD 2.61B by 2030, CAGR 16.7% (Nov 2024). — [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/sports-video-analytics-market-177635342.html)
- **Soccer camera market:** 7.9% CAGR 2025–2031. No $ figure is visible. Auto-focus and APAC are the fastest-growing segments. (Lucintel, May 2025) — [Lucintel](https://lucintel.com/soccer-camera-market.aspx)
- **Youth/grassroots streaming demand** (Native Frame × Reeplayer survey via Regen Sports, date not stated):
  - 22% of teams livestream, while 63% of non-streamers are interested.
  - Platforms used: Facebook Live 29%, **YouTube Live 27%**, GameChanger 9%.
  - Barriers to streaming: cost 50%, privacy 42%, staffing 38%, equipment 37%.
  - Concerns: video quality 60%.
  - Desired features: "device flexibility" 45%, auto highlights 37%.
  - The headline mentions a "$2.5B youth sports tech gap" with no stated method. — [Regen Sports](https://regensports.substack.com/p/the-25-billion-youth-sports-tech)
- **Pixellot volume:** 16,000 systems in 9,000 US schools, over 2.5M production hours in 2025, over 100K events per month, and about 100M views. — [Pulse 2.0, Jan 2026](https://pulse2.com/pixellot-35-million-closed-to-expand-ai-driven-youth-sports-streaming-and-monetization)

### Inferences
- Market-research numbers come from paid-report vendors with opaque methods. Treat them as directional only.
- The survey data is the most actionable:
  - Cost and equipment are the top barriers.
  - YouTube is already a top-2 destination.
  - "Device flexibility" is the most-wanted feature.
  - All three favor a phone-only, YouTube-streaming product.

### Gaps
- No credible, sourced figure for "amateur sports streaming" market size in $ found. The $37B youth-sports figure tied to NBC SportsEngine Play was seen in a headline only and not verified.
- No unit-sales data for Veo, XbotGo or BallerCam.

## 4. Gaps and complaints in user reviews

### Takeaway
The dominant complaints about incumbents are:
- subscription lock-in (a camera that's useless without a plan)
- recording-hour caps and add-on "nickel and diming"
- billing and cancellation problems
- poor support
- occasional tracking misses

Phone and lens products draw complaints about connectivity, sync and stream quality.

### Cited Findings
- Veo Trustpilot score is 4.0/5 from 1,293 reviews, but **34% are 1-star**. Complaints (Aug–Sep 2026):
  - users moved to Starter with a monthly cap, calling the camera "a paperweight that Veo can choose to brick mid-contract"
  - "Crazy to pay $2,000+ camera and run out of hours when playing 2-3 games a week"
  - "nickel and dimed"
  - charged for 2 years after leaving
  - support "non-existent", hidden behind chatbots
  - tracking occasionally fails to follow the ball
  - "Wish the video quality would be 4k" — [Trustpilot: Veo](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/veo.co)
- Hudl Focus: "you own the camera but need an active subscription to use it". — [Hudl FAQ](https://www.hudl.com/products/focus/faq/portable)
- Trace: a membership is now required to watch, plus upload failures, slow email-only support, and missed moments. — [App Store: Trace Teams](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1351001083)
- BallerCam: time sync across devices, poor connections degrading recordings, wrong clip segments, a broken desktop viewer, and requests for faster startup and stream pause. — [App Store: BallerCam](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6747689741)
- Veo Go: device compatibility (iPhone 17), no full-match download, and no live. — [Veo Go](https://www.veo.com/product/veo-go); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6475045572)
- XbotGo explicitly markets against "$3,000+" systems with monthly fees. — [XbotGo blog](https://xbotgo.com/fr/blogs/buying-guide/xbotgo-chameleon-review)

### Inferences
Exploitable positioning for a phone-only product:
- no hardware to brick
- pay-per-game or low annual pricing with no hour caps on local recording
- full-match download (Veo Go lacks it)
- direct YouTube live with your own channel and no proprietary viewer lock-in
- Android support
- a transparent cancellation process

### Gaps
- Reddit and forum sentiment was not captured, so the review evidence is limited to Trustpilot and App Store.

## 5. Funding, acquisitions, shutdowns 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Consolidation and continued funding:
- Hudl bought Balltime (Feb 2025) and Titan Sports.
- Pixellot raised $35M (Jan 2026).
- Spiideo raised $20M (Jun 2024).
- Veo shipped a phone product (Veo Go).

No confirmed shutdowns found.

### Cited Findings
- **Pixellot, 30 Jan 2026:** $35M ($15M equity from PSG plus $20M venture debt). This follows a $161M Series D in 2022. It also signed a 5-year extension with PlayOn Sports. — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/pixellot-35-million-closed-to-expand-ai-driven-youth-sports-streaming-and-monetization)
- **Hudl acquired Balltime, completed 6 Feb 2025:** price undisclosed. Balltime had 12,000+ teams and 125,000+ athletes. — [Hudl blog](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-expands-volleyball-focus-through-game-changing-acquisition-of-balltime)
  - The price was reportedly "8 figures". — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown)
  - Hudl also acquired Titan Sports (Jun 2025). — [Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/06/lincoln-based-hudl-acquires-titan-sports-and-balltime-expanding-access-to-performance-data-for-athletes/) (headline seen; article not fetched)
- **Spiideo, Jun 2024:** $20M growth round led by Cipio Partners. — [SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2024/06/06/ai-powered-swedish-sport-tech-spiideo-raises-20-million-in-growth-round-led-by-germanys-cipio-partners/)
- **Veo:** an aggregator lists total funding of $80M (Series C) and 201–500 employees, with the date unclear. — [Signalbase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/news/funding/veo-technologies-scores-80-million-in-series-c-to-revolutionize-sports-video-recording) (low-quality aggregator)
- **Trace:** last known round was a $47M Series C (Apr 2022, $65.2M total). — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/sports-video-tech-company-trace-closes-47m-funding-round/) (pre-2024)
  - In Aug 2026, Trace shifted to a family-paid team plan with a free camera lease. — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/traceup)
- **PlaySight** was acquired by Slinger for $82M (headline only; date likely 2021, pre-window). — [Israel Desks](https://israeldesks.com/?p=4077)

### Inferences
- Incumbents are moving downmarket. Veo Go, Trace's free camera lease and Pixellot's agentic AI all point that way, which signals price pressure on hardware-plus-subscription models.
- Balltime shows a solo/small-team AI app on phone footage can reach about $1M+ ARR and an exit within a few years (vertical: volleyball).

### Gaps
- No confirmed info on SoloShot, Pivo sports features, or any shutdowns in 2024–2026.
- Veo Go launch date not found.
- Veo layoffs and new funding not found.
